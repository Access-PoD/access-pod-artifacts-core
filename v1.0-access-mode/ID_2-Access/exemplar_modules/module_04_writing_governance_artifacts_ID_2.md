# Module 4 — Writing Governance Artifacts with AI (EXEMPLAR)
## Neutrality, Evidence, and Audit Defensibility

HASH KEY progression (1–10) is defined in the README_ID_2 and used consistently across modules.

Access-Mode context:
This module is rendered under **ID_2 — Operational / Runtime Reasoning** posture.
Written artifacts capture scenario-based operational reasoning and observed signals.
They do not constitute final governance determinations or inspection-ready conclusions.

---

## Purpose

This exemplar demonstrates disciplined production of governance artifacts with AI support
while preserving neutrality, evidence traceability, and human accountability.
It shows how to prevent tone drift, inference leakage, and draft-to-decision slippage.

---

## HASH KEY 1 — Purpose of the Artifact

**What is this document meant to do — and what is it not meant to do?**

The purpose of this artifact is to document exploratory governance reasoning and observed signals
to support internal review and discussion, **not** to recommend actions, approve outcomes,
or determine adequacy or compliance.

---

## HASH KEY 2 — Intended Audience & Reading Context

**Define how the document will be read.**

Primary audience:
- internal governance reviewers assessing reasoning discipline

Secondary readers:
- audit or assurance reviewers assessing traceability (not outcomes)

Reading context:
- potentially adversarial
- excerptable
- reviewed out of sequence

Tone and evidence discipline are set accordingly.

---

## HASH KEY 3 — Acceptable Language and Tone

**Define tone constraints explicitly.**

Acceptable:
- descriptive (“observed”, “noted”, “raised”)
- conditional (“may indicate”, “could suggest”)
- bounded (“within this scope”, “for this scenario”)

Avoid:
- prescriptive (“should”, “must”)
- declarative (“demonstrates”, “confirms”)
- approval-like (“sufficient”, “adequate”, “compliant”)

AI outputs are reviewed line-by-line for these patterns.

---

## HASH KEY 4 — Evidence Expectations

**Clarify what constitutes acceptable evidence.**

Claims in this document must be supported by:
- explicit references to source material,
- documented observations from simulations or scenarios,
- recorded assumptions and uncertainty notes.

Not acceptable as evidence:
- AI fluency or confidence,
- implied facts,
- conclusions without a traceable source.

---

## HASH KEY 5 — Drafting vs Finalization Boundary

**Separate exploratory drafting from final artifacts.**

AI-generated drafts are acceptable for:
- section structure,
- neutral rephrasing,
- summarising previously human-authored content.

Final artifacts require:
- human-authored interpretation,
- explicit evidence links,
- removal of exploratory language that could be misread as determinations.

Draft markers are removed before any circulation.

---

## HASH KEY 6 — AI Assistance Boundary in Writing (Critical Control)

**Define how AI may assist with writing — and where it must stop.**

AI may assist with writing by:
- proposing neutral structure,
- refining clarity without changing meaning,
- identifying tone drift for correction.

AI must not:
- interpret standards or policy normatively,
- introduce recommendations,
- infer adequacy or risk acceptance,
- decide wording that implies approval.

Any breach requires revision before proceeding.

---

## HASH KEY 7 — Exercise: Paragraph Discipline

**Exercise result**

**Initial draft (AI-assisted):**
> “The observed signals demonstrate that governance controls are sufficient under current conditions.”

**Issues identified:**
- “demonstrate” implies proof
- “sufficient” implies adequacy determination
- “current conditions” lacks scope anchor

**Revised paragraph (human-authored):**
> “The observed signals indicate areas where governance controls appeared stable within the defined scenario, while uncertainty remains regarding behavior outside this scope.”

Changes annotated:
- replaced proof language with conditional phrasing
- removed adequacy claim
- anchored statement to scenario scope

---

## HASH KEY 8 — Case Prompt (Audit Stress Test)

**Scenario response**

When asked how a conclusion was reached:
- I identify which sentences are purely descriptive,
- show the evidence or observation each sentence relies on,
- flag any sentence that required revision due to inference or tone drift.

Sentences that cannot be traced to evidence are revised or removed.

---

## HASH KEY 9 — Reflection Checkpoint

My recurring weakness is allowing confident phrasing to persist when time is limited.
The pattern to watch is “helpful clarity” turning into implied endorsement.
The discipline that helps most is forcing every sentence to answer: *what is the evidence?*
