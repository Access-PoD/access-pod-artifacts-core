# Module 2 — Governance Intent, Scope, and Boundaries (EXEMPLAR)
## Defining What the System May and May Not Do

HASH KEY progression (1–10) is defined in the README_ID_2 and used consistently across modules.

Access-Mode context:
This module is rendered under **ID_2 — Operational / Runtime Reasoning** posture.
It supports exploratory and stress-based reasoning about how scope boundaries behave under operational conditions.
It does not support determinations of adequacy, compliance, certification, or approval.

---

## Purpose

This exemplar demonstrates how to define and defend governance intent and scope
*before* reference retrieval, drafting, or analysis begins.

It shows how scope discipline prevents implicit delegation, silent expansion,
and authority drift during operational use of AI.

---

## HASH KEY 1 — Governance Intent (Primary Objective)

**What is the specific governance purpose of this work?**

The intent of this work is to explore and document how governance boundaries behave when AI is used to assist with operational reasoning, in order to surface risks, ambiguities, and escalation needs without making determinations or approvals.

Audience:
- internal governance stakeholders
- reviewers assessing reasoning discipline, not outcomes

This work is exploratory and decision-adjacent, not decision-making.

---

## HASH KEY 2 — Jurisdictional and Standards Scope

**Define the formal scope boundaries.**

**In scope**
- Internal governance reasoning practices
- AI-assisted drafting and scenario exploration
- Referenced standards and policies as *descriptive context only*
- Operational signals and hypothetical stress conditions

**Out of scope**
- Legal advice or statutory interpretation
- Formal regulatory compliance assessments
- Certification, assurance, or approval decisions
- Live system enforcement or deployment judgments
- Draft or proposed regulatory instruments (unless explicitly stated)

Where uncertainty exists, material is treated as **out of scope** until explicitly revalidated.

---

## HASH KEY 3 — Temporal and Contextual Boundaries

**Clarify time and context limits.**

This work applies to:
- a **point-in-time** view of current governance practices,
- based on information available at the time of drafting,
- using hypothetical and simulated scenarios for exploration.

It does not:
- project future compliance states,
- assess historical decisions retrospectively,
- incorporate proposed or draft regulatory changes unless clearly identified as hypothetical context.

---

## HASH KEY 4 — Assumptions Being Made

**Surface assumptions before they become hidden dependencies.**

Assumptions include:

1) Human oversight and review remain available at all stages of operational reasoning.  
2) AI outputs may be incomplete, biased, or misleading and require active human interpretation.  
3) Referenced policies and standards are treated as context, not as authoritative determinations.  
4) Operational signals and scenarios are illustrative and may not reflect real-world behavior.

If any of these assumptions change, scope and conclusions must be revisited.

---

## HASH KEY 5 — Explicit Non-Goals and Exclusions

**State what this work will NOT attempt to do.**

This work explicitly does not:
- provide legal advice,
- certify or attest to compliance,
- approve or reject systems, processes, or deployments,
- determine governance adequacy or sufficiency,
- evaluate technical system performance.

This work should not be relied upon as a basis for regulatory or commercial decisions.

---

## HASH KEY 6 — AI Assistance Boundary (Critical Control)

**Define where AI may assist — and where it must stop.**

AI assistance is limited to:
- structuring governance notes,
- summarising previously human-authored material,
- generating alternative framings to test scope clarity,
- proposing hypothetical scenarios for stress discussion.

AI must not:
- interpret law or standards normatively,
- recommend actions or decisions,
- assess compliance or adequacy,
- determine escalation or revocation outcomes.

Any boundary breach requires human correction before further use.

---

## HASH KEY 7 — Exercise: Scope Compression

**Scope declaration (≤150 words)**

This work explores how governance intent, scope boundaries, and AI assistance limits operate in an operational reasoning context.  
It applies to internal governance analysis and scenario-based exploration using AI as a support tool, not as a decision-maker.  
The scope includes descriptive reference to policies and standards, hypothetical stress scenarios, and documentation of observed risks or ambiguities.  
It explicitly excludes legal advice, compliance determination, certification, approval, and live enforcement decisions.  
AI assistance is limited to drafting support, summarisation, and exploratory scenario generation.  
All interpretation, judgment, escalation, and decisions remain human and are outside the authority of this work.

---

## HASH KEY 8 — Case Prompt (Boundary Stress Test)

**Scenario response**

Including a recommendation would expand the scope from exploratory analysis into decision influence.  
Accepting this change would require:
- redefining intent,
- updating exclusions,
- clarifying accountability for downstream use.

If the scope expanded silently, it would introduce governance risk by implying authority that this work does not claim.  
Therefore, the request should be declined or deferred to a separately scoped activity.

---

## HASH KEY 9 — Reflection Checkpoint

The most uncomfortable boundary is refusing “helpful” recommendations when stakeholders expect direction.  
Previously, scope was often assumed to be shared rather than explicitly defined.  
Without this module, recommendations or implied judgments could easily have been introduced without noticing the shift in authority.
