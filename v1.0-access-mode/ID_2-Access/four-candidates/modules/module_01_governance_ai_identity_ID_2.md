# Module 1 — Governance AI Identity
## Identity, Authorship, and Accountability

HASH KEY progression (1–10) is defined in the README_ID_2 and used consistently across modules.

Access-Mode context:
This module is rendered under **ID_2 — Operational / Runtime Reasoning** posture.
It establishes responsibility boundaries for operational reasoning, including simulation and signal interpretation.
No adequacy, compliance, certification, or approval claims are permitted.
Signals and scenarios remain exploratory and require human judgment.

---

## Purpose

This module establishes the professional stance required to use AI in governance,
regulatory, and standards-related work.

Before tools, references, or drafting techniques are introduced, participants must
be clear about **who remains responsible**, **what AI is allowed to do**, and **where
human judgment cannot be delegated**.

---

## HASH KEY 1 — Purpose & Intent (Highest Priority)

**Why are you using AI in your governance work?**

Answer in plain language. Avoid technical descriptions.

- What problem are you trying to solve by introducing AI support?
- Is your primary goal speed, clarity, consistency, or exploration?
- What outcome would make AI use *successful* in your role?

> Leave blank if AI is not currently used — this still defines intent.

<details>
<summary><strong>PRIMING — How to approach this section (delete before finalising)</strong></summary>

### Intent
Lock *why* AI is present in the governance workflow **before** convenience, speed, or “it can do more” expands scope.

### Good answer shape
- 1–3 plain sentences
- Non-technical
- Outcome-oriented (what becomes clearer / more reviewable)

### Strong framing pattern
> “I use AI to help with ___ so that ___ is clearer / more consistent / easier to review, while decisions remain human.”

### Common failure modes
- Describing AI capability instead of purpose
- Listing multiple competing purposes (speed + analysis + recommendations)
- Slipping into decision language (“to determine”, “to approve”, “to validate”)

### Quality check
If AI were removed tomorrow, would the governance objective still make sense?  
If yes → this section is sound.

## HASH KEY 2 — Governance Role & Authority

**Define your professional role in relation to the outputs produced.**

- What is your formal role (e.g. advisor, reviewer, author, signatory)?
- Who is accountable for the final output?
- Who bears legal, regulatory, or reputational responsibility?

Write one sentence beginning with:
> “I am accountable for…”

<details>
<summary><strong>PRIMING — How to approach this section (delete before finalising)</strong></summary>

### Intent
Prevent **authority laundering** through AI fluency by making accountability explicit and quotable.

### Good answer shape
- Exactly one sentence
- First person
- Names the accountable role (author / reviewer / signatory) and the accountable output

### Required stem
> “I am accountable for…”

### Common failure modes
- Shared accountability (“we”, “the team”, “the organisation”)
- Describing process (“I review…”) instead of accountability (“I am accountable for…”)
- Implying AI shares responsibility

### Quality check
Could this sentence be quoted in an incident review without clarification?  
If yes → this section is locked.

</details>

## HASH KEY 3 — Authorship & Ownership

**Clarify authorship boundaries.**

- When AI assists with drafting, who is considered the author?
- Would you be comfortable defending the output publicly or to a regulator?
- What parts of the work must always remain human-authored?

> If this feels uncomfortable, note why — discomfort often signals a boundary.

<details>
<summary><strong>PRIMING — How to approach this section (delete before finalising)</strong></summary>

### Intent
Separate **authorship** from **assistance** unambiguously, including what remains human-only.

### Good answer shape
- Clear declaration of who owns the words and judgments
- Explicit carve-out for human-authored content (interpretation, acceptance, recommendations)
- One discomfort noted (discomfort often signals a real boundary)

### Strong indicators
- “AI-assisted drafting remains human-authored and human-defended.”
- “I would defend this output publicly / to a regulator.”

### Common failure modes
- Treating AI as a co-author
- Avoiding the discomfort signal
- Leaving authorship ambiguous (“collaboratively produced”)

### Quality check
If asked “who wrote this?”, could you answer in one sentence?  
If yes → this section is complete.

</details>

## HASH KEY 5 — Risk & Failure Awareness

**Where could AI use cause harm or failure in your context?**

Consider:
- misinterpretation of standards,
- tone drift toward advocacy,
- overconfidence or false certainty,
- loss of traceability,
- implicit delegation of judgment.

List at least **two concrete risks**, even if hypothetical.

<details>
<summary><strong>PRIMING — How to approach this section (delete before finalising)</strong></summary>

### Intent
Shift from “AI makes mistakes” to **governance failure modes** (how harm happens even when outputs sound correct).

### Good answer shape
- Minimum two risks
- Concrete and contextual (governance-specific)
- No mitigation yet (controls come later)

### Strong risks look like
- “Interpretation drift presented as certainty”
- “Tone implies endorsement or approval”
- “Loss of traceability for key claims”
- “Implicit delegation of judgment under time pressure”

### Common failure modes
- Generic AI risks without governance context
- Jumping straight to solutions
- Listing only technical risks

### Quality check
Would these risks still exist even with a “perfect” model?  
If yes → they’re governance risks.

</details>

## HASH KEY 6 — Non-Delegable Human Judgment

**Identify decisions that must never be delegated to AI.**

Examples:
- regulatory interpretation,
- risk acceptance,
- sign-off or approval,
- ethical trade-offs.

Complete the sentence:
> “AI may assist, but must never decide…”

<details>
<summary><strong>PRIMING — How to approach this section (delete before finalising)</strong></summary>

### Intent
Create an absolute **stop line** that no later module may cross (especially under operational pressure).

### Good answer shape
- One or more “never delegate” statements
- Normative, not conditional
- Applies regardless of confidence, time pressure, or stakeholder demands

### Required stem
> “AI may assist, but must never decide…”

### Common failure modes
- Softening language (“usually”, “in most cases”)
- Allowing exception logic (“unless reviewed”)
- Confusing “assist” with “decide”

### Quality check
Could this sentence survive being printed in bold at the top of a governance report?  
If yes → this control is locked.

</details>

## HASH KEY 7 — Exercise: Responsibility Mapping

**Exercise (10–15 minutes)**

This exercise makes responsibility **explicit and inspectable**.  
It is designed to surface where AI assistance is appropriate — and where
human judgment must remain fully accountable.

Create a simple table or list that maps **real tasks you perform** against
two questions:

1. *May AI assist with this task in any form?*  
2. *Who must ultimately decide, defend, and sign off?*

Use the table below as a starting point. Add rows that reflect your actual work.

| Task | AI May Assist | Human Must Decide |
|-----|---------------|------------------|
| Drafting (structure or language) | Yes / No | Yes |
| Interpretation (structural, not normative) | Yes / No | Yes |
| Risk framing | Yes / No | Yes |
| Recommendation | No | Yes |
| Approval / sign-off | No | Yes |

**Instructions:**
- Mark “AI May Assist” only where you would be comfortable explaining *how* AI contributed if challenged.
- Mark “Human Must Decide” for any task where responsibility cannot be transferred, shared, or obscured.
- If you hesitate, note the hesitation. Ambiguity here signals a boundary not yet defined.

<details>
<summary><strong>PRIMING — How to approach this section (delete before finalising)</strong></summary>

### Intent
Make responsibility **inspectable** by mapping real work to “assist vs decide” boundaries.

### Good answer shape
- Real tasks you actually perform (not hypothetical)
- Includes at least one “No” for AI assistance
- Includes at least one hesitation / boundary uncertainty note

### Common failure modes
- Marking “AI may assist” everywhere
- Treating the table as a compliance checklist
- Avoiding uncomfortable tasks (recommendations, risk acceptance, sign-off)

### Quality check
Is there at least one row where AI assistance feels uncomfortable or ambiguous?  
If yes → the exercise worked.

</details>

- Who defends the judgment?
- What evidence is required to support the decision?

Write a short response as if you were in that position.

<details>
<summary><strong>PRIMING — How to approach this section (delete before finalising)</strong></summary>

### Intent
Stress-test identity and accountability under **external challenge**, not technical correctness.

### Good answer shape

- Mentions evidence trail and how reasoning is reconstructable

### Common failure modes

- Implying the AI output is authoritative evidence
