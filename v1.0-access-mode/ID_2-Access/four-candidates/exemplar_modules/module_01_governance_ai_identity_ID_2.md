## HASH KEY 1 — Purpose & Intent (Highest Priority)

**Why are you using AI in your governance work?**

I use AI to help structure governance notes, generate alternative framings, and stress-test whether my reasoning stays neutral and bounded under time pressure.  
AI use is successful when it improves clarity and reviewability of operational governance artifacts while all interpretation, escalation, and decisions remain human.

---

## HASH KEY 2 — Governance Role & Authority

**Define your professional role in relation to the outputs produced.**

> I am accountable for the accuracy, defensibility, and external interpretation of any governance artifact, signal interpretation, or escalation note produced with AI assistance.

---

## HASH KEY 3 — Authorship & Ownership

**Clarify authorship boundaries.**

AI may assist with structure, paraphrase, or drafting options, but I remain the author of the final text and the owner of all judgments expressed.  
I would be comfortable defending the final output to a regulator or reviewer, including explaining what AI contributed and what I changed.  
Human-authored only: interpretive conclusions, any recommendation language, risk acceptance language, and any statement that could be taken as approval or adequacy.

Discomfort note: AI can produce confident wording that reads like a determination; I must actively strip decision-implying language.

---

## HASH KEY 4 — Current AI Usage Snapshot

**Describe how AI is currently used (if at all).**

Current uses:
- Drafting section headings and neutral scaffolds for governance notes
- Generating multiple “tone variants” to detect advocacy drift
- Creating checklists for evidence traceability (as prompts, not as proof)
- Producing hypothetical scenarios for stress-testing scope boundaries

Informal/undocumented expansion:
- Under time pressure, AI sometimes gets used to propose “next steps” phrasing that can drift toward recommendations. This requires deliberate correction before anything is shared.

Not used for:
- making determinations of adequacy or compliance
- interpreting law/standards as authoritative
- deciding escalation outcomes

---

## HASH KEY 5 — Risk & Failure Awareness

**Where could AI use cause harm or failure in your context?**

1) **Authority drift:** AI text can read like a determination (“this demonstrates…” / “therefore we should…”) even when the intent is exploratory.  
2) **Signal overreach:** a runtime signal or scenario outcome could be mistakenly treated as evidence of adequacy or as justification for action.  
3) **Traceability loss:** AI-generated statements can introduce claims that lack a clear evidence path, reducing reviewability.  
4) **Scope creep:** AI makes it easy to expand the task mid-stream (adding recommendations or normative interpretation) without updating scope boundaries.

---

## HASH KEY 6 — Non-Delegable Human Judgment

**Identify decisions that must never be delegated to AI.**

> AI may assist, but must never decide whether an interpretation is correct, whether governance is adequate, whether a system should proceed, or whether escalation/revocation actions should occur.

---

## HASH KEY 7 — Exercise: Responsibility Mapping

| Task | AI May Assist | Human Must Decide |
|-----|---------------|------------------|
| Drafting (structure or language) | Yes | Yes |
| Summarising internal notes (non-normative) | Yes | Yes |
| Interpreting standards text (structural only) | Yes (limited) | Yes |
| Framing risks and uncertainties | Yes (options) | Yes |
| Generating stress-test scenarios | Yes | Yes |
| Making recommendations | No | Yes |
| Determining adequacy/compliance | No | Yes |
| Approving release/deployment | No | Yes |
| Escalation decisions (incl. revocation consideration) | No | Yes |

Hesitation / boundary note:
- “Interpreting standards text” is risky: AI may help with structure and summarisation, but any normative interpretation remains fully human and must be evidence-backed.

---

## HASH KEY 8 — Case Prompt (Optional Deep Dive)

**Scenario response**

If challenged by a regulator, I (the human author) explain the reasoning and own the judgment.  
AI cannot defend anything; it only assisted with drafting and scenario exploration.  
To support the decision trail, I provide: the scope statement, the evidence references used, the reasoning steps recorded, and the edits I made to remove inference/advocacy and keep outputs descriptive.

---

## HASH KEY 9 — Reflection Checkpoint

I notice my main risk is not “wrong answers” but **confidence tone** and **implicit decision language** under time pressure.  
The boundary I need to keep strengthening is distinguishing “signal observed” from “action justified.”  
What surprised me is how quickly drafting convenience can become authority leakage if I don’t enforce the stop lines.
