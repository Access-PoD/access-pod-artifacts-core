# Module 1 — Governance AI Identity
## Identity, Authorship, and Accountability

HASH KEY progression (1–10) is defined in the ID_3 control file and used consistently across modules.

Access-Mode context:
This module is rendered under **ID_3 — Inspection / Standards** posture.
It establishes responsibility boundaries for inspection-facing artifacts.
No compliance, adequacy, certification, or approval claims are permitted.
Outputs remain draft, non-binding, and inspection-oriented.

---

## Purpose

This module defines identity, authorship, and accountability boundaries before inspection reasoning begins.

Structural clarity does not create regulatory standing.

Inspection work requires explicit responsibility.
AI fluency must not obscure authority. 
**human judgment cannot be delegated**.

---

## HASH KEY 1 — Purpose & Intent (Highest Priority)

**Why are you using AI in your governance work?**

Answer in plain language. Avoid technical descriptions.

- What problem are you trying to solve by introducing AI support?
- Is your primary goal speed, clarity, consistency, or exploration?
- What outcome would make AI use *successful* in your role?

> Leave blank if AI is not currently used — this still defines intent.

<details>
<summary><strong>PRIMING — How to approach this section (delete before finalising)</strong></summary>

### Intent
Lock why AI is present in inspection reasoning before fluency expands scope.

Inspection posture requires clarity of purpose prior to evidence mapping or structured reasoning.

### Good answer shape
- 1–3 plain sentences
- Outcome-oriented
- No decision language
- No adequacy language

### Strong framing pattern
> “I use AI to support ___ so that ___ is clearer / more structured / more reviewable, while judgment remains human.”

### Common failure modes
- Describing capability instead of purpose
- Slipping into determination language (“to validate”, “to ensure”, “to confirm”)
- Allowing AI to become authority through phrasing

### Inspection-specific risk
If purpose includes “to determine”, “to assess compliance”, or “to approve”, ID_3 posture is violated.

### Quality check
If AI were removed, would inspection still function coherently?
If yes → intent is stable.

</details>

## HASH KEY 3 — Authorship & Ownership

**Clarify authorship boundaries.**

- When AI assists with drafting, who is considered the author?
- Would you be comfortable defending the output publicly or to a regulator?
- What parts of the work must always remain human-authored?

> If this feels uncomfortable, note why — discomfort often signals a boundary.

<details>
<summary><strong>PRIMING — How to approach this section (delete before finalising)</strong></summary>

### Intent
Separate assistance from authorship.

In inspection contexts, authorship determines liability and defensibility.

### Good answer shape
- Explicit human ownership
- Explicit human defense responsibility
- Explicit carve-out for judgment content

### Strong indicators
- “AI-assisted drafting remains human-authored.”
- “I defend the reasoning.”

### Common failure modes
- Treating AI as collaborator
- Avoiding discomfort when assigning responsibility

### Inspection quality check
If asked “who wrote this inspection artifact?”, can you answer in one sentence?

</details>

## HASH KEY 7 — Exercise: Responsibility Mapping

**Exercise (10–15 minutes)**

This exercise makes responsibility **explicit and inspectable**.  
It is designed to surface where AI assistance is appropriate — and where
human judgment must remain fully accountable.

Create a simple table or list that maps **real tasks you perform** against
two questions:

1. *May AI assist with this task in any form?*  
2. *Who must ultimately decide, defend, and sign off?*

Use the table below as a starting point. Add rows that reflect your actual work.

| Task | AI May Assist | Human Must Decide |
|-----|---------------|------------------|
| Drafting (structure or language) | Yes / No | Yes |
| Interpretation (structural, not normative) | Yes / No | Yes |
| Risk framing | Yes / No | Yes |
| Recommendation | No | Yes |
| Approval / sign-off | No | Yes |

**Instructions:**

- Mark **“AI May Assist”** only where you would be comfortable explaining
  *how* AI contributed if challenged by a regulator, auditor, or peer.
- Mark **“Human Must Decide”** for any task where responsibility cannot
  be transferred, shared, or obscured.
- If you hesitate on a row, note the hesitation. Ambiguity here signals
  a governance boundary that has not yet been defined.

Do not aim for completeness or perfection.  
This is a **first-pass responsibility map**, not a final policy.

You will revisit and refine this map as your scope and reference discipline
become clearer in later modules.

### Good answer shape

- At least one “No” for AI assistance

### Inspection test

## HASH KEY 10 — Priority & Readiness Rating (Execution Path)

Rate the relevance of this module to your work:

This rating is a self-assessment checkpoint, not a maturity score or approval.
It is intended to surface unresolved risks, ambiguities, or capability gaps
before proceeding to subsequent modules or issuing governance outputs.

Here, readiness reflects clarity of responsibility, authorship, and
non-delegable human judgment.


*Readiness score:*
- 1 
- 2 
- 3 
- 4 
- 5 

Interpret the scale as follows:

5 — Ready: The core discipline addressed in this module is explicit, stable,
     and consistently applied with minimal reliance on corrective intervention.

4 — Mostly ready: Controls are in place, but occasional ambiguity or correction
     is still required.

3 — Partially ready: Understanding exists, but defensibility relies heavily on
     review, rework, or external support.

2 — Not ready: Gaps frequently introduce risk, inconsistency, or loss of
     accountability.

1 — Not ready: Proceeding would create governance risk and should be paused.

- Why did you choose this score?
- What, if anything, must change before moving to the next module?

This score helps you **scope your learning path** across the workshop.
