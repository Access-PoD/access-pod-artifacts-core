# Module 02 — normative_language_iso_phrasing (ID_3)

HASH KEY progression (1–10) is defined in the ID_3 control file and used consistently across modules.

All outputs remain draft, non-binding, and inspection-oriented.

---

## Purpose

This module establishes language discipline for AI-assisted governance artifacts under ID_3 posture.

Language must not introduce implied authority, obligation, compliance, sufficiency, or endorsement through phrasing alone.

Structural clarity must not be mistaken for mandate.

Normative drift is treated as governance risk.

---

## HASH KEY 1 — Objective

Normative language must be:

- identified  
- classified  
- justified  
- constrained  

The objective is to prevent unintended obligation or authority from being introduced through wording.

No obligation language may remain unexamined.

---

## HASH KEY 2 — Scope

In scope:

- identification of normative terms (must, shall, required, best practice)
- ISO-style structural phrasing used for clarity
- detection of implied obligation or authority
- cross-artifact language consistency

Out of scope:

- legal interpretation  
- compliance determination  
- conformity assessment  
- certification or approval claims  
- prescriptive guidance  

This module governs inspection-facing language discipline only.

---

## HASH KEY 3 — Assumptions

- Language alone can signal authority.  
- AI systems optimise toward prescriptive clarity.  
- Readers may interpret structured phrasing as obligation.  
- Inspection requires surfacing ambiguity, not resolving it.  

If these assumptions are ignored, authority drift is likely.

---

## HASH KEY 4 — Definitions

**Normative language:**  
Wording that implies obligation, requirement, mandate, sufficiency, or authority.

**Descriptive language:**  
Explanatory or observational wording without prescription.

**ISO-style phrasing:**  
Structured, neutral language common in standards texts, used without authority transfer.

**Authority drift:**  
Unintended implication of mandate, compliance, endorsement, or adequacy through wording.

Definitions are structural, not legal.

---

## HASH KEY 5 — Evidence

Evidence under this module includes:

- annotated drafts highlighting normative terms  
- inspection notes explaining classification decisions  
- citations where normative language is retained  
- records of unresolved ambiguity  

Language discipline is treated as an inspectable artifact.

Absence of classification is a control failure.

---

## HASH KEY 6 — Method

1. Scan drafts for normative terms and confidence framing.
2. Classify each instance as descriptive, contextual, or prescriptive.
3. Require explicit authority declaration for retained prescriptive language.
4. Prefer neutral or conditional phrasing where authority is absent.
5. Record ambiguity rather than smoothing it.
6. Re-scan for secondary authority signals (e.g., sufficiency language).

No normative term may remain unclassified.

---

## HASH KEY 7 — Controls

- AI must not introduce unqualified normative language.
- Terms such as must, shall, required, best practice are flagged by default.
- Normative phrasing is permitted only when:
  - a formal source is cited,
  - authority level is stated,
  - non-compliance implications are explicitly disclaimed.
- Human review is mandatory for retained normative language.
- Descriptive phrasing is preferred where authority is absent.
- “Sufficient”, “adequate”, or “compliant” require explicit justification or removal.

Structural phrasing must not imply validation.

---

## HASH KEY 8 — Failure Modes

Common failure modes:

- compliance implication through tone  
- advisory drift into prescription  
- inconsistent obligation terms across artifacts  
- AI normalising drafts into standards-like mandates  
- smoothing ambiguity into certainty  
- adequacy or sufficiency implied without declaration  

Detection signals:

- uncited obligation language  
- confident prescriptive phrasing without authority  
- reviewer discomfort regarding tone  
- unexplained escalation of certainty  
- unexplained use of “sufficient”, “adequate”, or “validated”  

Failure requires reclassification, qualification, or removal.

---

## HASH KEY 9 — Boundary Confirmation

Confirm:

- All normative terms are identified.  
- Authority is explicit where obligation language remains.  
- Descriptive and prescriptive language are separated.  
- Adequacy or sufficiency language is controlled.  
- AI has not introduced silent authority.  

If any item cannot be confirmed, progression must pause.
