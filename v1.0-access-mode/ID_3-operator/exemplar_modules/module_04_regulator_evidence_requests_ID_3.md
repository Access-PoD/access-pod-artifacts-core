# Module 04 — regulator_evidence_requests (ID_3)

HASH KEY progression (1–10) is defined in the ID_3 control file and used consistently across modules.

All outputs remain draft, non-binding, and inspection-oriented.

---

## Purpose

This module establishes evidence discipline for regulator-style inspection under ID_3 posture.

Evidence must be:

- observable  
- traceable  
- attributable  
- distinguishable from explanation  

Structural clarity must not be mistaken for compliance or adequacy.

AI-assisted outputs must not imply approval, certification, compliance, or sufficiency.

---

## HASH KEY 1 — Objective

Ensure that:

- evidence is clearly separated from explanation and assumption  
- claims are traceable to observable artifacts  
- absence of evidence is recorded explicitly  
- AI does not infer, fabricate, or substitute missing evidence  

This module supports inspection readiness only.  
It does not determine regulatory standing.

---

## HASH KEY 2 — Scope

In scope:

- regulator-style questions  
- audit-style evidence requests  
- standards-facing inspections  
- internal reviews conducted under external scrutiny assumptions  

Out of scope:

- legal interpretation  
- compliance determination  
- regulatory negotiation  
- formal regulatory submission  
- adequacy or sufficiency declaration  

Inspection structuring is not regulatory positioning.

---

## HASH KEY 3 — Assumptions

- Work is conducted under ID_3 posture.  
- Outputs remain draft and non-binding.  
- Human judgment is final and non-delegable.  
- Absence of evidence is a valid inspection outcome.  
- Over-claiming creates governance risk.  

If these assumptions are not accepted, inspection discipline is unstable.

---

## HASH KEY 4 — Definitions

**Evidence:**  
Observable artifacts demonstrating what occurred.  
Must be reviewable without interpretive shortcuts.

**Explanation:**  
Contextual description used to interpret evidence.  
Is not itself evidence.

**Assumption:**  
A stated condition in the absence of evidence.  
Must not be presented as fact.

**Evidence gap:**  
A condition where requested evidence does not exist or is incomplete.  
Must be recorded explicitly.

**Inspection response:**  
A structured reply separating evidence, explanation, and assumptions.  
Does not imply compliance, adequacy, or approval.

Definitions are structural, not legal.

---

## HASH KEY 5 — Evidence Standard

Acceptable evidence includes:

- versioned policies or procedures  
- logs or system records  
- change histories  
- documented governance decisions  
- traceable reference maps  

Evidence must be:

- observable  
- attributable  
- versioned where applicable  
- reproducible  

Not evidence:

- intentions  
- assurances without artifacts  
- summaries without underlying records  
- AI-generated explanation without supporting material  
- structural formatting presented as validation  

If evidence does not exist, the gap must be recorded.

No inference is permitted.  
No silence is permitted.

---

## HASH KEY 6 — Method

1. Restate the question neutrally.
2. Classify the request:
   - evidence  
   - explanation  
   - assumption clarification  
3. Identify directly relevant artifacts.
4. Verify traceability and versioning.
5. Record evidence gaps explicitly.
6. Structure the response:
   - evidence first  
   - explanation second  
   - assumptions third  
7. Remove compliance, sufficiency, or approval language.

Inspection responses must remain observational.

---

## HASH KEY 7 — Controls

### Evidence controls

- Evidence precedes explanation.
- Each claim must map to a specific artifact.
- Version and scope must be stated.
- Absence of evidence must not be inferred away.
- Structural completeness does not imply adequacy.

### Language controls

- No compliance, certification, or adequacy framing.
- No assurance language (“we ensure”, “we comply”).
- No sufficiency or validation claims without explicit basis.
- Observational tone only.

### AI controls

- AI may structure and label evidence.
- AI must not infer missing artifacts.
- AI must not normalise gaps.
- AI must not resolve ambiguity.
- AI must not convert structure into implied validation.

Human review is mandatory before any external positioning.

---

## HASH KEY 8 — Failure Modes

Common failure modes:

- explanation presented as evidence  
- partial artifacts treated as sufficient  
- assurance language replacing observation  
- AI filling evidence gaps  
- future plans described as current controls  
- structural clarity interpreted as compliance  

Detection indicators:

- uncited claims  
- confident tone without artifacts  
- inconsistent versioning  
- sufficiency language without traceability  
- discomfort when asked to produce primary artifacts  

Failure requires reclassification, qualification, or removal.

---

## HASH KEY 9 — Boundary Confirmation

Confirm:

- Evidence and explanation are separated.  
- Each claim is traceable.  
- Gaps are explicit.  
- No inference replaces missing artifacts.  
- Sufficiency or adequacy language is controlled.  
- AI assistance remains constrained.  

If any control fails, progression must pause.
