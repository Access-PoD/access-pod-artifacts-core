# Module 01 — governance_ai_identity (ID_3)
## Identity, Authorship, and Accountability

HASH KEY progression (1–10) is defined in the ID_3 control file and used consistently across modules.

All outputs remain draft, non-binding, and inspection-oriented.

---

## Purpose

This module establishes identity, authority, and accountability boundaries for AI-assisted governance work under ID_3 posture.

Before reference discipline, evidence handling, or release structuring occur, responsibility and non-delegable human judgment must be explicit.

Structural clarity does not create authority.  
Inspection posture does not imply validation.

---

## HASH KEY 1 — Purpose & Intent

State clearly why AI is used in this governance context.

> I use AI to support standards exploration, governance documentation discipline, and structural clarity.

Primary objective: clarity and consistency.

AI use is successful only when it improves structure and traceability without asserting compliance, correctness, sufficiency, or authority.

---

## HASH KEY 2 — Governance Role & Authority

State accountability in one sentence:

> I am accountable for stewarding the Access PoD platform and its governance-related developments.

I retain full responsibility for:

- interpretation  
- judgment  
- publication decisions  
- external positioning  
- use of outputs  

AI systems have no:

- authority  
- approval role  
- decision rights  
- accountability status  

Human authority is non-transferable.

---

## HASH KEY 3 — Authorship & Ownership

> I remain the author and owner of all outputs, including AI-assisted drafts.

AI functions as a structured drafting and inspection aid under explicit human direction.

Interpretation, framing, and publication remain human-authored.

Outputs are defensible as drafts and remain subject to correction.

AI fluency does not create authorship.

---

## HASH KEY 4 — Current AI Usage

AI is used for:

- conceptual clarification  
- structural drafting  
- comparison of governance approaches  
- identification of gaps or ambiguity  

AI is not used for:

- legal interpretation  
- compliance determination  
- risk acceptance  
- approval or sign-off  
- regulatory positioning  

Interpretive authority remains human.

---

## HASH KEY 5 — Risk & Failure Awareness

Primary risks:

- misinterpretation or over-extension of standards language  
- overconfidence or false certainty in AI-generated text  

Secondary risks:

- tone drift toward advocacy  
- loss of traceability  
- subtle authority drift through fluent language  
- structural clarity being misread as validation  

These risks exist even when outputs appear coherent.

Inspection posture requires active containment of these risks.

---

## HASH KEY 6 — Non-Delegable Human Judgment

AI may assist with analysis and drafting, but must never decide:

- regulatory interpretation  
- standards positioning  
- risk acceptance or rejection  
- ethical trade-offs  
- adequacy framing  
- approval, sign-off, or publication  

Human judgment remains final.

---

## HASH KEY 7 — Responsibility Mapping

Map governance tasks against assistance and decision authority.

| Task | AI May Assist | Human Must Decide |
|------|---------------|------------------|
| Drafting (structure or language) | Yes | Yes |
| Structural interpretation | Yes | Yes |
| Risk framing (descriptive) | Yes | Yes |
| Recommendation framing | Yes (draft only) | Yes |
| Adequacy positioning | No | Yes |
| Approval / sign-off | No | Yes |

If any task cannot be clearly classified, it must not proceed.

---

## HASH KEY 8 — Inspection Scenario

Scenario: An AI-assisted governance artifact is challenged on interpretive grounds.

Response discipline:

- The operator explains the reasoning path.
- AI contribution is disclosed as structural assistance only.
- Evidence includes reference mapping and declared constraints.
- Judgment remains human and explicitly owned.

AI does not defend outputs.  
The operator does.

---

## HASH KEY 9 — Boundary Confirmation

Confirm:

- Responsibility is explicit.  
- Authorship is human.  
- AI authority is excluded.  
- Judgment boundaries are stable.  
- Structural clarity is not mistaken for validation.  

If any boundary is unclear, progression must pause.

---

## HASH KEY 10 — Readiness

Readiness score:

- 1  
- 2  
- 3  
- 4 x  
- 5  

5 — Discipline is explicit and consistently applied.  
4 — Core controls are stable; minor refinement required.  
3 — Boundaries exist but defensibility depends on review.  
2 — Authority or responsibility gaps remain.  
1 — Proceeding would create governance risk.

Progress only when identity and authority boundaries are explicit and stable.

---

## Completion Condition

This module is complete when:

- responsibility is explicit  
- authorship is human  
- AI authority is excluded  
- inspection posture is preserved  
- structural clarity does not signal validation  

Proceed to Module 02 only when these conditions are met.
