---

## Purpose

This module focuses on **producing governance artifacts** with AI support
*without compromising neutrality, evidentiary discipline, or accountability*.

In governance contexts, the primary risk is not incorrect language —
it is **tone drift**, **implicit recommendation**, or **unverifiable reasoning**.

This module defines how AI may assist in drafting while ensuring that
final outputs remain **human-authored, defensible, and auditable**.

---

## HASH KEY 1 — Purpose of the Artifact

**What is this document meant to do — and what is it not meant to do?**

Answer clearly before drafting begins.

- Is this an assessment, explanation, summary, or record?
- Is it decision-support or decision-adjacent?
- Who will rely on it?

> The purpose of this artifact is to describe and organise governance considerations for learning and review, not to recommend actions or assert adequacy.

---

## HASH KEY 2 — Intended Audience & Reading Context

**Define how the document will be read.**

- Regulator?
- Internal committee?
- External reviewer?
- Public-facing?

Consider:
- scrutiny level,
- adversarial potential,
- expectation of neutrality.

> This document is written for internal reviewers and learners and is expected to be read as a descriptive, non-authoritative governance artifact.

---

## HASH KEY 3 — Acceptable Language and Tone

**Define tone constraints explicitly.**

Examples:
- neutral, non-advocacy,
- descriptive, not persuasive,
- evidence-referenced, not speculative.

List:
- phrases or styles to avoid,
- phrases or constructions that are acceptable.

> Language in this artifact must remain neutral, descriptive, and assumption-explicit. It must avoid endorsement language, advocacy, or implied conclusions.

---

## HASH KEY 4 — Evidence Expectations

**Clarify what constitutes acceptable evidence.**

- direct citation only?
- paraphrase with reference?
- internal policy cross-reference?

> Claims in this artifact must be supported by cited source material or clearly stated assumptions. Explanatory text must not be presented as evidence.

---

## HASH KEY 5 — Drafting vs Finalization Boundary

**Separate exploratory drafting from final artifacts.**

- What is allowed in early drafts?
- What must be removed or tightened before finalization?

> AI-generated drafts are acceptable for structuring sections, exploring alternative phrasing, and improving clarity in early working drafts, provided all content remains provisional and subject to human review.

> Final artifacts require explicit confirmation of scope and assumptions, verification of all claims against cited sources, removal of any implied recommendations or adequacy language, and human authorship of all judgment-bearing statements.

---

## HASH KEY 6 — AI Assistance Boundary in Writing (Critical Control)

**Define how AI may assist with writing — and where it must stop.**

Examples:
- structuring sections,
- language refinement,
- summarisation.

Explicitly exclude:
- interpretation,
- recommendation,
- risk acceptance.

> AI may assist with outlining, rewriting for clarity, and summarising provided text. AI must not interpret standards, recommend actions, or decide final wording of judgments.

---

## HASH KEY 7 — Exercise: Paragraph Discipline

**Exercise (20–25 minutes)**

1. Draft one governance paragraph with AI assistance.
2. Review it line by line and identify:
   - implied recommendation,
   - unreferenced claims,
   - tone drift.
3. Rewrite the paragraph to remove all three.

> Draft paragraph:
“This approach aligns with best practice and ensures strong governance.”

> Issues identified: implied endorsement (“best practice”), unsubstantiated claim.

> Rewritten paragraph:
“This approach is described to illustrate how governance considerations may be structured. No claim is made regarding adequacy or alignment with external standards.”

---

## HASH KEY 8 — Case Prompt (Audit Stress Test)

Case prompts are optional and intended for deeper stress-testing where time permits.

**Scenario**

A reviewer asks:
> “Can you show how this conclusion was reached?”

- Which sentences are defensible?
- Which rely on inference?
- What evidence trail exists?

Write a short response indicating:
- what you can defend,
- what you would revise.

> Defensible sentences are those that describe structure or sources. Any sentence implying adequacy would be revised or removed unless supported by explicit authority.

---

## HASH KEY 9 — Reflection Checkpoint

Reflect:

- Where does your writing tend to drift toward opinion?
- Which sentences feel hardest to defend?
- What does “neutral” actually mean in your domain?

> My tendency is to tighten language until it sounds confident. I need to slow down and check whether confidence is warranted or merely stylistic.
