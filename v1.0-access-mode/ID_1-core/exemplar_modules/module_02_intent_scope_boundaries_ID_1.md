## Defining What the System May and May Not Do

## Purpose

This module establishes **governance intent and scope discipline** before reference retrieval,
drafting, or analysis begins.

Most governance failures involving AI do not arise from incorrect answers — they arise from
**unclear scope**, **unstated assumptions**, or **implicit delegation of authority**.

---

## HASH KEY 1 — Governance Intent (Primary Objective)

**What is the specific governance purpose of this work?**

Answer in one or two sentences.

- Are you assessing risk, explaining policy, supporting a decision, or documenting compliance?
- Is this work exploratory, advisory, or decision-adjacent?
- Who is the intended audience (regulator, internal committee, public)?

> The intent of this work is to define and document learning-oriented governance scope and boundaries for how AI may assist with drafting and understanding. It is written for internal use in a learning workspace so that assumptions and limitations remain explicit and reviewable.

---

## HASH KEY 2 — Jurisdictional and Standards Scope

**Define the formal scope boundaries.**

- Which jurisdictions apply?
- Which standards, frameworks, or regulatory instruments are in scope?
- Are drafts, guidance notes, or informal interpretations included or excluded?

List explicitly:
- **In scope**
- **Out of scope**

> In scope: Conceptual governance scoping discipline; high-level reference to applicable frameworks/standards categories as labels; documenting what is included/excluded in this learning workspace.
> Out of scope: Jurisdiction-specific legal interpretation; determination of obligations; standards conformance claims; compliance assessments; certification or approval conclusions; any “best practice” claims not tied to an explicitly cited authority.

## HASH KEY 4 — Assumptions Being Made

**Surface assumptions before they become hidden dependencies.**

Consider:
- assumed system behavior,
- assumed data quality,
- assumed compliance posture,
- assumed human oversight.

1. We assume this work is used as a learning scaffold and may be revised as understanding improves.

2. We assume any referenced standards or frameworks are treated as categories for orientation, not as authoritative interpretations.

3. We assume human review remains responsible for all judgments, including what is in/out of scope and how outputs are reused.

4. We assume AI assistance is limited to drafting support and clarification, not decision-making or recommendation.

## HASH KEY 5 — Explicit Non-Goals and Exclusions

**State what this work will NOT attempt to do.**

Examples:
- Not providing legal advice
- Not certifying compliance
- Not approving deployment
- Not evaluating technical performance

Complete:
> This work explicitly does not provide legal advice, certify compliance, approve deployment, or evaluate technical system performance. It also does not make final judgments of adequacy, sufficiency, or readiness.

## HASH KEY 6 — AI Assistance Boundary (Critical Control)

**Define where AI may assist — and where it must stop.**

For this specific task:
- Can AI assist with structuring?
- With summarisation?
- With language refinement?

Identify at least **one boundary** where AI must not proceed without human intervention.

> AI assistance is limited to structuring text, summarising provided material, and refining language for clarity within the declared scope. AI must not interpret standards, infer obligations, recommend actions, or expand scope without explicit human intervention and revalidation.

---

## HASH KEY 7 — Exercise: Scope Compression

**Exercise (15–20 minutes)**

Write a **scope declaration** of no more than 150 words that includes:
- intent,
- scope,
- exclusions,
- AI assistance limits.

> The intent of this work is to establish clear learning-oriented governance scope and boundaries for using AI assistance in drafting and concept clarification. Scope includes defining what topics are included/excluded, making assumptions explicit, and documenting limits on AI assistance. Out of scope are legal interpretations, determination of obligations, compliance or certification claims, approval or readiness language, and recommendations. AI assistance is limited to structuring, summarising provided text, and neutral language refinement; AI must not decide scope, interpret standards, or recommend actions. This scope statement exists to prevent drift and to ensure all outputs remain interpretable, challengeable, and human-led.

## HASH KEY 8 — Case Prompt (Boundary Stress Test)

Case prompts are optional and intended for deeper stress-testing where time permits.

**Scenario**

Midway through drafting, a stakeholder asks:
> “Can we also include a recommendation?”

- Is this within scope?
- What changes if you say yes?
- What governance risk is introduced if the scope silently expands?

> That request (adding a recommendation) is out of scope for this artifact as currently defined, because it shifts the work from clarification into decision-adjacent guidance. If we want to include recommendations, we need to update the intent and exclusions, restate AI assistance limits, and revalidate assumptions before continuing. Otherwise, we should keep this document descriptive and boundary-focused.

---

## HASH KEY 9 — Reflection Checkpoint

Reflect honestly:

- Where was scope previously assumed rather than defined?
- Which exclusions were uncomfortable to write?
- What would have gone wrong without this module?

Note any resistance — resistance usually signals a real boundary.

> The hardest exclusion to write cleanly is “recommendation,” because it often feels like a natural next step after clarification.

> The biggest drift risk is that “clarifying” can quietly become “interpreting,” especially with standards language.

> The most protective boundary is limiting AI to structure/summarise/rewrite, while keeping obligation and decision language human-only.
