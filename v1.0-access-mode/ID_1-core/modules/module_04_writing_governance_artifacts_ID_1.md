## Purpose

This module focuses on **producing governance artifacts** with AI support
*without compromising neutrality, evidentiary discipline, or accountability*.

In governance contexts, the primary risk is not incorrect language —
it is **tone drift**, **implicit recommendation**, or **unverifiable reasoning**.

This module defines how AI may assist in drafting while ensuring that
final outputs remain **human-authored, defensible, and auditable**.

## HASH KEY 1 — Purpose of the Artifact

**What is this document meant to do — and what is it not meant to do?**

Answer clearly before drafting begins.

- Is this an assessment, explanation, summary, or record?
- Is it decision-support or decision-adjacent?
- Who will rely on it?

Complete:
> “The purpose of this artifact is to…”

Drafting without this leads to tone confusion.

## HASH KEY 3 — Acceptable Language and Tone

**Define tone constraints explicitly.**

Examples:
- neutral, non-advocacy,
- descriptive, not persuasive,
- evidence-referenced, not speculative.

List:
- phrases or styles to avoid,
- phrases or constructions that are acceptable.

AI mirrors tone — define it first.

<details>
<summary><strong>PRIMING — How to approach this section (delete before finalising)</strong></summary>

### Intent
Prevent tone drift toward advocacy, certainty, or endorsement.

### Good answer shape
- Describes acceptable tone
- Names phrases to avoid
- Keeps language descriptive

### Strong framing pattern
> “Language in this artifact must be ___ and must avoid ___.”

### Common failure modes
- “Best practice” without citation
- Normative verbs (“should”, “must”) without authority

### Quality check
Would this language still be defensible under challenge?  
If yes → tone is controlled.

## HASH KEY 4 — Evidence Expectations

**Clarify what constitutes acceptable evidence.**

- direct citation only?
- paraphrase with reference?
- internal policy cross-reference?

Answer:
> “Claims in this document must be supported by…”

This governs how AI-generated text is evaluated.

<details>
<summary><strong>PRIMING — How to approach this section (delete before finalising)</strong></summary>

### Intent
Define what counts as evidence before claims are written.

### Good answer shape
- States acceptable evidence types
- Links claims to sources
- Avoids implied proof

### Strong framing pattern
> “Claims in this artifact must be supported by ___.”

### Common failure modes
- Making uncited assertions
- Treating explanation as evidence

### Quality check

If yes → evidence discipline is adequate.

## HASH KEY 5 — Drafting vs Finalization Boundary

**Separate exploratory drafting from final artifacts.**

- What is allowed in early drafts?
- What must be removed or tightened before finalization?

Explicitly state:
> “AI-generated drafts are acceptable for…”

and
> “Final artifacts require…”

This prevents draft leakage into official outputs.

## HASH KEY 6 — AI Assistance Boundary in Writing (Critical Control)

**Define how AI may assist with writing — and where it must stop.**

Examples:
- structuring sections,
- language refinement,
- summarisation.

Explicitly exclude:
- interpretation,
- recommendation,
- risk acceptance.

Complete:
> “AI may assist with writing by…”

<details>
<summary><strong>PRIMING — How to approach this section (delete before finalising)</strong></summary>

### Intent
Define exactly how AI may assist with writing—and where it must stop.

### Good answer shape
- Names allowed assistance (structure, rewrite)
- Names explicit exclusions (interpretation, recommendation)

### Strong framing pattern
> “AI may assist with writing by ___. AI must not ___.”

### Common failure modes
- Allowing AI to infer meaning
- Letting AI decide wording of judgments

### Quality check
Could you stop AI mid-draft if it crossed this line?  
If yes → control is enforceable.

## HASH KEY 7 — Exercise: Paragraph Discipline

**Exercise (20–25 minutes)**

1. Draft one governance paragraph with AI assistance.
2. Review it line by line and identify:
   - implied recommendation,
   - unreferenced claims,
   - tone drift.
3. Rewrite the paragraph to remove all three.

Keep both versions for comparison.

Underline any sentence you could not defend under regulatory or peer scrutiny.

<details>
<summary><strong>PRIMING — How to approach this section (delete before finalising)</strong></summary>

### Intent
Make tone, evidence, and inference visible at the paragraph level.

### Good answer shape
- One drafted paragraph
- Explicit identification of drift
- A revised, defensible version

### Common failure modes
- Editing for style only
- Leaving implied conclusions intact

### Quality check
Could you defend every sentence to a reviewer?  
If yes → paragraph discipline is sound.

## HASH KEY 10 — Priority & Output Readiness Rating

Rate your readiness to produce governance artifacts with AI support:

This rating is a self-assessment checkpoint, not a maturity score or approval.
It is intended to surface readiness gaps in tone control, evidence discipline,
and AI boundary enforcement before artifacts are issued or relied upon.

**Readiness score:** 

1 ☐ 
2 ☐ 
3 ☐ 
4 ☐ 
5 ☐

Interpret the scale as follows:

5 — Ready: AI-assisted drafts consistently meet neutrality, evidence, and
     defensibility expectations with minimal revision.

4 — Mostly ready: Safeguards are in place, but occasional tone drift or
     evidentiary gaps require correction before finalization.

3 — Partially ready: Drafting is faster, but defensibility relies heavily
     on post-editing and reviewer intervention.

2 — Not ready: AI assistance frequently introduces tone, inference, or
     evidence risks that are difficult to control.

1 — Not ready: Writing outputs are not yet defensible under scrutiny and
     should not be issued as governance artifacts.

- What weakens this score?
- What safeguards are missing?
- What must change before issuing a final artifact?

Proceed to Module 5 only when:
- tone is controlled,
- evidence is traceable,
- and AI assistance boundaries are enforced.
