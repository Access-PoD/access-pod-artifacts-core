# Module 6 — Forward Practice, Standards Evolution, and Reflection
## Sustaining Governance Literacy Without Chasing Hype

HASH KEY progression (1–10) is defined in the README and used consistently across modules.

---

## Purpose

This module consolidates learning from the previous modules and focuses on
**ongoing professional practice** rather than completion.

AI governance is not a static discipline. Standards evolve, systems change,
and new governance models emerge. The challenge is not keeping up with
everything, but **engaging selectively, responsibly, and with confidence**.

---

## HASH KEY 1 — Purpose of Continued Learning

**Why must governance learning continue after this workshop?**

Answer honestly.

- What will change in your environment over the next 12–24 months?
- Which risks increase if learning stops?
- What type of ignorance would be most harmful in your role?

Complete:
> “Continued learning matters in my role because…”

---

## HASH KEY 2 — Signals of Meaningful Change

**Distinguish signal from noise.**

Consider:
- regulatory changes,
- new standards drafts,
- shifts in enforcement expectations,
- emerging system behaviors.

List:
- **signals that require attention**
- **noise that can be safely ignored**

This is a professional survival skill.

---

## HASH KEY 3 — Personal Governance Literacy Boundary

**Define what you must understand deeply vs what you only need awareness of.**

Examples:
- deep understanding of standards you apply,
- high-level literacy in adjacent domains,
- no obligation to master tooling.

Complete:
> “I need deep literacy in…”

and
> “I only need awareness of…”

---

## HASH KEY 4 — Safe Experimentation Zones

**Identify where you can learn without risk.**

Examples:
- sandbox scenarios,
- internal simulations,
- non-binding drafts,
- peer discussion forums.

List:
- safe experimentation areas,
- areas where experimentation is not acceptable.

This preserves trust while enabling growth.

---

## HASH KEY 5 — Contribution vs Adoption

**Clarify how you engage with emerging governance models.**

- Do you contribute to discussions?
- Observe pilots?
- Adopt only after validation?

Write:
> “My default posture toward new governance models is…”

This avoids premature commitment.

---

## HASH KEY 6 — Professional Integrity Check (Critical Control)

**Re-anchor integrity as systems evolve.**

Ask:
- Would I stand behind this approach publicly?
- Can I explain it without hiding behind tools?
- Does this preserve accountability?

Complete:
> “I will disengage from an approach if…”

This is your ethical backstop.

---

## HASH KEY 7 — Exercise: Forward Practice Plan

**Exercise (20 minutes)**

Draft a simple 6–12 month practice plan:

- One area to deepen
- One area to monitor
- One area to explicitly ignore
- One forum or source to engage with thoughtfully

Revisit Module 2 when scope drift or uncertainty reappears.

Keep this realistic.

## HASH KEY 8 — Case Prompt (Future Challenge)

Case prompts are optional and intended for deeper stress-testing where time permits.

**Scenario**

A new governance framework gains attention in your field.
Peers ask:
> “Are you using this yet?”

- How do you respond responsibly?
- What would you need to evaluate before engaging?
- What risks exist in staying silent vs rushing in?

Write a short response.

## HASH KEY 9 — Reflection Checkpoint

Reflect on the full workshop:

- Which module shifted your thinking the most?
- Where did resistance appear?
- What now feels clearer or calmer?

These are indicators of learning, not gaps.

## HASH KEY 10 — Confidence & Readiness Rating (Closure)

Rate your confidence in continuing governance practice with AI support:

This rating is a self-assessment checkpoint, not a maturity score or approval.
It is intended to surface unresolved risks, ambiguities, or capability gaps
before proceeding to subsequent modules or issuing governance outputs.

Here, readiness reflects confidence in sustaining governance literacy and
judgment as systems, standards, and expectations evolve.

**Confidence score:** 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 5 ☐

Interpret the scale as follows:

5 — Ready: The core discipline addressed in this module is explicit, stable,
     and consistently applied with minimal reliance on corrective intervention.

4 — Mostly ready: Controls are in place, but occasional ambiguity or correction
     is still required.

3 — Partially ready: Understanding exists, but defensibility relies heavily on
     review, rework, or external support.

2 — Not ready: Gaps frequently introduce risk, inconsistency, or loss of
     accountability.

1 — Not ready: Proceeding would create governance risk and should be paused.

- What reinforces this confidence?
- What still feels unresolved?
- What support would you seek next?

## Module 6 Completion Note

This workshop is complete when:

- AI is positioned as a support, not a substitute,
- governance remains human-led,
- learning is intentional, not reactive.

You do not leave with answers.
You leave with **clarity, boundaries, and a sustainable practice**.
