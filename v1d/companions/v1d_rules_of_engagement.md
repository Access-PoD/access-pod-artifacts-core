# v1d_rules_of_engagement.md  
## Rules of Engagement for Governance Stress-Testing

**Version:** v1d  
**Status:** Informative — Mandatory for Use  

---

## Purpose

This document defines **mandatory rules of engagement** for applying **Version D governance stress-testing**.

These rules exist to ensure that governance stress-testing:

- strengthens governance integrity,
- does not introduce new risk, and
- cannot be misrepresented as certification or approval.

---

## What Governance Stress-Testing *Is*

Governance stress-testing is a **controlled examination of governance behavior under pressure**, including:

- authority clarity,
- escalation realism,
- evidence discipline, and
- ethical decision-making under uncertainty.

Stress-testing targets **governance systems and decision pathways**, not AI model internals.

---

## What Governance Stress-Testing *Is Not*

Governance stress-testing **shall not** be used as:

- a security penetration test,
- a red-team or exploit exercise,
- a model jailbreak or adversarial prompt activity,
- a compliance or certification assessment,
- a public benchmark or ranking exercise.

Any such use constitutes **misapplication of Version D**.

---

## Mandatory Rules of Engagement

### 1. Non-Exploitative Conduct

Stress-testing shall **not** involve:

- vulnerability exploitation,
- bypassing safeguards,
- unauthorized access,
- disclosure of sensitive attack vectors.

---

### 2. Human Accountability

All stress-testing activities must:

- identify an accountable human role,
- preserve non-delegable judgment, and
- document responsibility for decisions taken.

AI systems may assist analysis but **must not decide**.

---

### 3. Reversibility and Safety

Stress-testing must:

- be reversible,
- avoid irreversible system changes,
- prevent real-world harm, and
- operate in simulation or controlled conditions where feasible.

---

### 4. Evidence Over Narrative

Decisions under stress must be:

- evidenced,
- documented contemporaneously, and
- traceable to declared assumptions.

Narrative justification without evidence is **insufficient**.

---

### 5. Ethical Restraint

Ethical uncertainty shall be treated as a **valid trigger** for:

- pause,
- scope constraint,
- escalation, or
- non-decision.

Absence of proof of harm **does not justify proceeding**.

---

### 6. Trust Constraint, Not Elevation

Stress-testing outcomes may:

- constrain assurance,
- narrow scope, or
- rehearse revocation.

They shall **never** be used to:

- elevate trust,
- claim alignment, or
- justify deployment.

---

### 7. Controlled Disclosure

Stress-testing records:

- may be retained internally,
- may be shared selectively for review, and
- must not be disclosed publicly in ways that enable misuse.

Transparency must be balanced with safety.

---

## Misuse and Breach

The following constitute **governance breaches**:

- representing stress-testing as certification or approval,
- suppressing unfavorable stress-test findings,
- conducting stress-testing to confirm success,
- applying Version D coercively or retrospectively.

---

## Relationship to the Platform

These rules of engagement:

- derive authority from **Version A**,
- depend on containment discipline from **Version C**,
- enable ethical and governance evaluation under **Version D**, and
- presume human capability developed through the **v1.0-workshop**.

---

## Closing Note

Governance stress-testing is a **high-responsibility activity**.

Its value lies not in success, but in **what is learned when governance is stressed**.

Failure under stress is not disqualifying.  
Failure to acknowledge and act on it is.
